## Bottom line

**The candidate-8 result does NOT replicate out of sample. FAIL on the pre-registered primary test: on grid families GENCO's
pretraining never saw (RTE, PEGASE, Polish, SimBench-HV; 17 grids), zero-shot GENCO beats the best of five learning-free
interpolators for unmeasured-bus |V| at 40% measured on 8 of 17 grids (needed 12, i.e. 2/3).** Secondary: with 0.5% measurement
noise at 40% it wins 2 of 17 (FAIL); at 20% noise-free 3 of 17 (FAIL). At 10% it wins 0 of 17.

The family breakdown is the finding. At 40% noise-free: Polish 3/3, SimBench-HV 4/6, RTE 1/5, PEGASE 0/3. GENCO loses
clearly on the large RTE and PEGASE grids (error 1.5x to 2.1x the best interpolator on case6470/6495/6515rte, case8387/9241
pegase). On the **seen** family (GOC and EPIGRIDS, where pretraining used subgrids), reported separately and not in the
verdict, GENCO wins **15 of 15 at 40%** (13/15 at 20%, 11/15 at 10%), by large margins (ratios 0.18 to 0.9). So the advantage
GENCO shows looks like in-distribution knowledge of grid families it was pretrained on, not a general voltage estimator.
Noise hurts GENCO more than the interpolators: on the seen family 40%+noise falls from 15 to 8 wins.

Candidate 8's 12/15 at 40% on the pglib locked-15 should therefore be read as specific to those grids (several of which are
small pglib/IEEE cases close to what pretraining saw), not as evidence that GENCO beats classical interpolation on new grids.

## What was done

Pre-registered in `protocol.json` (commit 76797a1, before any GENCO-vs-interpolator comparison on these grids). Same five
interpolators I1-I5 as candidate 8 (code imported unchanged except I5's implementation, see caveats), same GENCO zero-shot with
candidate 7's mask, same metric (|V| MAE at unmeasured buses vs the TRUE |V|, median per grid over scenario x seed).
Conditions: 10/20/40% measured x 3 seeds, plus 0.5% Gaussian noise on every measured |V| (GENCO input and interpolators alike,
same noise draws) at 20% and 40% x 3 seeds. Ratio = median GENCO / min over I1..I5 of median (best re-selected inside every
resample), scenario-cluster bootstrap 2,000 resamples, win = ratio < 1 and CI upper < 1. PASS = wins on >= 2/3 of the primary
grids that ran.

**Grids that ran: all 32 of 32** (17 primary: RTE case1888/2848/6470/6495/6515; PEGASE case2869, 8387, 9241; Polish case2383wp,
3012wp, 3375wp; SimBench-HV 1-HV-mixed--0/1/2, 1-HV-urban--0/1/2. Seen: GOC 13 grids, 500 to 9591 buses; EPIGRIDS 5658, 7336).
Nothing was dropped. Scenario counts per grid: 100 (<= 3500 buses; 99 for the two sources that only have 100), 51 for larger grids
(scenario cap for time; the first 102/52 scenarios of each verified tree were staged and graphkit's split with test_ratio 0.99
gave the test set). 2,510 test scenarios in all.

Checks: visible |V| passes through GENCO unchanged in the noise-free runs (deviation 0.0 on every grid); with noise the input
noise has std 1.003 sigma (measured on a batch of case1888rte, max 3.75 sigma), identical to the noise applied to the interpolators;
GENCO noise runs are deterministic and do not compound across repeated runs; I5's scalable implementation equals
candidate 8's to 1e-13 where both apply; all 15 configs complete for every method on every grid.

## Observations and surprises

- **The seen/unseen split is the story.** 15/15 vs 8/17 at the same fraction on the same metric. GOC and EPIGRIDS grids are
  also where GENCO is far ahead (ratios down to 0.18). Because the seen set is a different set of grids (size, voltage structure),
  this is a strong association, not a clean causal test of "pretraining exposure"; an unseen-subgrid-of-seen-family control was not run.
- **GENCO is unusually bad on large unseen grids**: PEGASE 1.1x to 2.1x (never close to winning), RTE 1.3x to 1.8x except case1888rte (0.94, a narrow win).
  On Polish and the SimBench-HV urban grids it does win at 40%, but SimBench-HV mixed grids are ties (1.03 to 1.06 at 40%).
- **Noise matters.** At 40% with 0.5% noise the primary set drops from 8 to 2 wins, and even the seen family from 15 to 8. The harmonic-type
  interpolators average the noise out over many neighbours; GENCO passes the noisy measurements through as if exact (its output at measured buses equals the input).
- **The best classical baseline changes with the grid.** I4 (the load-aware correction) is best on 17 of 32 grids at 40% (the big RTE/PEGASE grids
  and many GOC), I2 on the Polish and SimBench grids; the GP (I5) on 3, I2 on 12. I4 did not help on the pglib grids in candidate 8, so
  a load-aware correction is worth something on the large, heavily loaded grids.
- 10% measured is hopeless for GENCO on unseen families (0/17), consistent with candidate 8 (6/15 there).

## Caveats

- **I5 differs from candidate 8 on grids with > 300 measured buses**: the marginal likelihood for (eps, lam) is evaluated on a seeded random
  subset of 300 measured buses (the posterior mean still uses all of them, via an exact sparse identity). Pre-registered; it affects
  I5 only (best of five on a minority of grids), so it can only make the classical bar slightly weaker, if at all.
- Scenario cap and graphkit's scenario ordering: the staged scenarios are the first K ids of each verified tree (assumed unordered with respect to load
  level; the verified trees were produced by random load sampling). Large grids have 51 scenarios, so their bootstrap CIs are narrow
  but rest on few scenarios; scenarios within a grid are not independent draws of the grid.
- Grids within a family are siblings (5 RTE, 6 SimBench-HV, 3 PEGASE): the 17-grid count overstates independent evidence in both directions.
- The primary condition (40%) was chosen because it was the passing secondary in candidate 8, i.e. the most favourable to GENCO; it still fails.
- All results are unaudited; verified-ladder label correctness is taken from the coordinator's statement and the converter logs, not re-checked here
  beyond the conversions' own stats.

## What was not done

- No control with unseen subgrids of the seen families, no GENCO finetuning or few-shot, no WLS state-estimation baseline.
- Candidate 7's dispatch-assuming AC baselines were not run on these grids.
- No audit. Scenario caps (100 / 51) rather than the full verified sets.
