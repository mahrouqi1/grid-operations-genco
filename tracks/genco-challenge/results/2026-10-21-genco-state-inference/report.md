# Candidate 7: zero-shot state inference with unknown dispatch, |V| at 20% of buses (2026-10-21)

> **UNAUDITED.** Produced by the lead chat; no `/audit` round has cleared it. Do not quote the numbers until one has.

## Bottom line

**FAIL on the pre-registered test: GENCO won 0 of 15 grids (needed 10).** The released pretraining checkpoint is clearly
worse than the classical alternative at branch angles on all 15 grids (ratio to the best of B1/B2/B3 between 1.37 and 8.2,
every CI above 1; it beats even the trivial flat-angle null on only 11 of 15 grids and loses to it on 4), and worse at
generator Pg on 14 of 15 (CI above 1; case3120 1.03 [1.02, 1.06]), with case1354 a tie (0.99 [0.97, 1.02]) (corrected per audit R1-F5). It does win on **|V| at unmeasured buses on 13 of 15
grids** (ratio 0.07 to 0.90, CI below 1), but that metric is not the claim: the classical baselines B1 to B3 are given
nothing that lets them use the 20% measurements at PQ buses, and a post-hoc two-line predictor that does use them
(harmonic interpolation of the measured |V| over the branch graph, `exploratory_vm.py`, NOT pre-registered) beats GENCO's |V| on 4 of 15 grids
(case73, 240, 300, 3120) and is within about 1.1x to 1.7x on several others (table below). So the |V| result says "GENCO
interpolates measured voltages about as well as a trivial smoother", not "GENCO infers the state without the dispatch".
Branch angle sets the line flows, so the claim "infers the full state better" does not hold.

## Setup (see `protocol.json`, committed before the GENCO run, commit f04c02f)

- Checkpoint: released pretraining `best_model_state_dict.pt`, no training, graphkit default normalizer path (no normalizer
  file; same as the 15-grid zero-shot scoring), OPF (pretraining) decoder, one deterministic pass.
- Scenarios: each grid's graphkit test split, identical to the 15-grid zero-shot scoring (counts match
  `2026-10-07-genco-heldout-zeroshot`); 2,398 scenarios in all.
- Task mask (`run_genco.py: DetMask`): Pg masked at every generator; Qg masked; Vm visible at exactly
  k = max(1, round(0.2 n)) seeded buses, masked elsewhere; Va masked except REF, which is **visible at its label value**
  (decision: the case's reference angle is input data of any power-flow problem; the baselines get it too); Pd, Qd, type
  flags, GS/BS, limits, costs visible; branch P/Q masked. Per-feature semantics follow graphkit `AddPretrainMask`
  (p_high on Vm/Va/Pg, Qg always masked) but deterministic, with p_low = 0.
- Baselines (`common.py: baselines`): use the scenario's own Ybus, branch and gen tables, loads, type flags, REF angle, and
  the case file (base Pg, Vg, base-case loss fraction). B1 = base dispatch scaled to total Pd x (1 + loss fraction), case-file
  Vg setpoints, AC Newton from a DC start. B2 = B1 with measured |V| imposed at measured PV/REF buses. B3 = DC with B1's
  dispatch. Also oracle dispatch (control) and flat null (Vm 1.0, Va = REF angle).
- Metrics per scenario: branch-angle-difference MAE (deg, all in-service branches), |V| MAE on unmeasured buses (pu), Pg MAE
  over in-service generators (MW; secondary). Per grid: median over scenarios; ratio to the best of B1/B2/B3 (re-selected inside
  each of 2,000 scenario-bootstrap resamples). Win = ratio < 1 and CI upper < 1, on both primary metrics.

## Controls

- **Oracle dispatch** (label Pg and label |V| at PV/REF, AC Newton): reproduces the labels to <= 7e-8 deg branch angle and
  <= 1e-9 pu in |V| on 14 of 15 grids wherever Newton converged. On **case240_pserc, Newton from the DC start fails to
  converge on 52 of 174 scenarios** (the labels themselves satisfy the power-flow equations to 3e-11 pu, checked directly, so
  this is the start, not the data); those fall back to the DC state, hence the 30.6 deg maximum in `baselines_per_scenario.jsonl`.
  The same non-convergence hits B1/B2 on case240 (83 and 84 of 174, fallback to DC, flagged `converged=false`); B3 is
  unaffected and is the best baseline there on |V| and Pg. No other grid had a B1/B2 failure.
- Oracle per-generator Pg is not exact on grids with several generators per bus (24, 73, 240, 2736, 3120): the oracle recovers
  bus-level Pg exactly, and the split across co-located generators uses the case-file weights. This affects the Pg column of
  the baselines in the same way (they have no better split either); GENCO predicts per generator directly.
- **GENCO with Vm visible at 100% of buses** (`genco_det100`): the branch-angle median is essentially unchanged on 8 grids (ratio
  det100/det20 between 0.99 and 1.02, e.g. case14 1.952 vs 1.951), better with full Vm on 5 (ratios 0.87 to 0.98) and *worse* on
  6 (case1354 1.99 vs 1.14, case2736 0.54 vs 0.36, case3120 1.44 vs 1.03, case240 8.4 vs 6.9, case57, case162). Pg MAE is within 5% on
  every grid. Giving GENCO every |V| does not rescue angles or Pg, so the failure is not a shortage of voltage information
  (and it suggests the angle head ignores Vm largely). Per-grid values: `genco_per_scenario.jsonl`.
- Visible entries pass through GENCO unchanged (max deviation 0.0 for visible |V| and REF angle on all grids); graph generator
  targets equal the datakit `gen_data` Pg (max difference 1.4e-3 MW, float32).

## Surprises and caveats

- Angles are the failure. GENCO's branch-angle median error is 0.36 to 6.9 deg across the 15 grids, the best classical 0.07 to 4.7
  deg; GENCO loses even to the *flat* angle null (all angles = REF angle) on case39, 240, 300 and 3120.
- Pg: GENCO's Pg MAE is 1.03 to 6.6x the best baseline (0.99 on case1354, a tie); the baselines' Pg is the case-file
  dispatch scaled to the load, which is itself a poor dispatch (labels come from a cost-permuted OPF), so the bar is low and
  GENCO still does not clear it.
- Base-case loss fraction (`common.py: base_case`) needed an amendment during the run (below); it is a 0.3 to 6.6% number that
  scales the whole B1 dispatch, so B1/B2 are sensitive to it by up to a few percent of total generation, but B3 (DC) is the
  best baseline on angles for most grids and that is dominated by the angle gap, which is 1.4x to 8x.
- Case-file Vg setpoints are all 1.0 pu on these pglib files, far from the label setpoints, so B1/B2/B3 are weak on |V|.
- The measured set is shared by the mask and the baselines (same seed function), but B1/B3 ignore it by definition.
- Single-checkpoint, single mask seed (the mask is deterministic by design), so the scenario bootstrap is the only variance source;
  scenarios within a grid are not independent draws of the grid (one topology family per grid).

## Amendments after the protocol commit (made before any baseline-vs-GENCO comparison was computed)

1. `base_case`: pandapower failed to solve the case file as given on case39 (file dispatch is 3,360 MW against 6,254 MW load).
   The loss fraction is now taken from the AC solution with every generator scaled by sum(Pd)/sum(Pg_file), applied to all grids
   (the case14 smoke had used the unscaled file); if that still fails (case162, case240) it falls back to a DC I^2 R estimate.
   This changed B1/B2's dispatch scale slightly, not the metric definitions or the verdict rule. No GENCO numbers were consulted.
2. Before the protocol commit, a smoke run of GENCO on case14 alone was seen (disclosed in `protocol.json`).

## What was not done

- No state-estimation baseline (weighted least squares over unknown Pg and Vm) and no baseline that fits the dispatch to the
  measured |V|; these are the stronger classical alternatives and could only widen the gap on angles. A B2 that also used measured
  PQ |V| as soft constraints is likewise absent.
- No unmasked-REF-angle variant, no multiple mask seeds or fractions (5%, 50%), no pretraining-mask (random) comparison.
- No audit.
