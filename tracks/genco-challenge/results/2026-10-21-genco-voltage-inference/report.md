# Candidate 8: is GENCO a better zero-shot |V| estimator than proper classical interpolators? (2026-10-21)

> **UNAUDITED.** Produced by the lead chat; no `/audit` round has cleared it. Do not quote the numbers until one has.

## Bottom line

**FAIL on the pre-registered primary test: at 20% observation (3 seeds pooled) GENCO beats the best of five learning-free
interpolators on 8 of 15 grids (needed 10).** Secondary: 10% FAIL (6 of 15), 40% **PASS (12 of 15)**. The picture is a
clean dependence on how much is measured: GENCO's advantage over classical interpolation grows with the measured fraction
(6 -> 8 -> 12 wins), i.e. GENCO uses measurements *better* than a smoother does once there are enough of them, but it is
no better than a good smoother when measurements are sparse. It is **not** a consistent win either way: on three
grids GENCO loses at every fraction by a wide margin (case240_pserc 3.6x to 4.9x the best interpolator's error, case300
2.3x to 2.8x, case3120 1.4x to 1.9x), and on case73, 118, 2736 it is a tie-or-loss at 20%. The point-estimate-only
count at 20% is 10 of 15, but the CI rule (fixed in advance) is what decides, and four of those ten are within a few
percent (case14 0.95 [0.90, 1.00], case118 0.98 [0.96, 1.02]).

Caveats before reading further: (a) the 20%/seed-0 GENCO numbers and the I1 (= #7's "B5") comparison were already known
from candidate 7, so the test is not a blind confirmation; (b) case5_pjm has 5 buses, so 10% and 20% are one measured bus
and every interpolator degenerates to a constant, which GENCO beats; (c) the same 15 grids generated the hypothesis.

## What was done

Protocol: `protocol.json` (commit 038ee1e, committed before any interpolator or GENCO run at the new configs; only a
mechanics smoke ran first). Five learning-free methods, all given loads and the measured |V| and nothing else: I1 harmonic
(unit weights), I2 harmonic (|Y_ij| weights), I3 nearest measured bus by electrical distance, I4 I2 plus a per-scenario
two-coefficient load-drop correction fitted on out-of-fold residuals at measured buses, I5 graph-kriging GP with
(eps, noise) chosen per scenario by marginal likelihood on the measured buses. GENCO (G) is the released checkpoint with
candidate 7's mask and inference code, unchanged. Configs: 10%, 20% (3 mask seeds), 40%. Metric: |V| MAE at unmeasured buses
per scenario, median per grid; ratio = G / best interpolator (re-selected in every one of 2,000 scenario-bootstrap
resamples; the three seeds of a scenario move together). Win = ratio < 1 and CI upper < 1.

Checks: G at 20%/seed 0 reproduces candidate 7 on all 2,398 scenarios to max |diff| 1.7e-5 pu (GPU float noise); visible
|V| passes through GENCO unchanged (deviation 0.0 on every grid and config); row counts complete (2,398 x 5 configs for G,
x 5 methods x 5 configs for interpolators).

## Observations and surprises

- **Admittance-weighted harmonic (I2) is the right classical bar.** It is the best of five on 10 of 15 grids at 20% and 12
  at 40%. The fancier methods did not help: the GP (I5) is best only on case14 and case1354 and by small margins; the
  load-aware I4 fitted a nonzero correction on 59% of scenarios (the rest had < 10 measured buses) with median
  coefficients ~0.0003 and -0.0046 pu per 100 MVA, and is no better than I2 (worse on case57: 0.0259 vs 0.0190 pu). So
  "load awareness" is not where the signal is; geometry is.
- **GENCO fails on large-voltage-spread grids.** case240_pserc (GENCO 0.091 vs I2 0.022 pu) and case300_ieee (0.046 vs
  0.018) are grids where candidate 7 already saw GENCO lose to every baseline; here the gap to interpolation is 2.5x to 5x
  and does not close at 40% (GENCO barely improves, 0.0912 to 0.0743 on case240). Conversely GENCO improves much faster
  with more measurements on most others (case39 0.0118 to 0.0063, case89 0.0093 to 0.0035).
- **case3120sp_k gets worse relative to I2 as fraction rises** (ratio 1.94, 1.39, 1.36) but never wins; case2736sp_k flips
  to a (narrow) win only at 40% (0.91).
- Seed variance matters for the verdict: single-seed wins at 20% are 7, 7, 6, pooled 8. Several grids sit at ratio
  0.9 to 1.1, so the 20% count is not stable to the exact mask; 10 or more would not have been reached by any single seed
  or the pooled set.
- Candidate 7's claim that GENCO "beats the baselines on |V| on 13 of 15" is, as suspected, mostly the baselines ignoring
  measurements: those baselines have an error of 0.02 to 0.09 pu while I2 gets 0.004 to 0.02.

## Proposed claim wording (pre-registered for FAIL, applied)

Zero-shot, the released GENCO checkpoint does not beat the best of five learning-free graph interpolators at estimating
unmeasured-bus |V| from sparse measurements: it wins on 8 of 15 grids at 20% (needed 10) and 6 of 15 at 10%; it does win
on 12 of 15 at 40%. Candidate 7's apparent |V| advantage over assumed-dispatch baselines reflects those baselines ignoring
the measurements. Unaudited.

## What was not done

- Candidate 7's assumed-dispatch baselines were not re-run at 10%, 40% or seeds 1-2 (reference column is 20%/seed 0 only).
- No state-estimation (WLS) baseline, no interpolator that uses the Ybus power-flow physics (e.g. fitting PV setpoints), and no
  GENCO finetuning or few-shot; the claim is zero-shot vs learning-free only. A stronger classical bar than I1-I5 might exist.
- I5 hyperparameter grids were small and fixed; a wider grid might change its rank, though it was best on only 2 grids.
- No audit. Fractions other than 10/20/40 and non-uniform sensor placement were not tested (measured sets are uniform random).
