## Bottom line

**Within one generator (the ladder generator: load-only perturbation, PV |V| fixed), the pre-registered size contrast is NOT SHOWN at the primary
setting: carved pieces of the unseen families are won by GENCO on 11 of 17 at 40%, against 8 of 17 for the ladder-generated unseen whole grids
(one-sided Fisher p = 0.245, needed < 0.05).** At 20% it is present: 10 of 17 pieces vs 3 of 17 whole grids (p = 0.016). So the earlier
whole-vs-piece gap (candidate 9: 8/17 whole; candidate 11: 14/17 datakit pieces) is **partly a generator effect**: the same 36 pieces
regenerated with the ladder generator give 11/17 instead of 14/17 for the unseen families at 40%, and the same pieces differ in 3 of 36 win flags at 40% (all 3 flips go datakit-win to ladder-lose,
none the other way; at 20% 4 of 36 differ, 2 each way). A residual piece advantage survives at 20% but not at 40%.

A second result, pre-registered as a secondary test, points the other way: **under the ladder generator the family contrast at piece size becomes
clear: seen-family pieces win 19/19 and unseen-family pieces 11/17 at 40% (two-sided Fisher p = 0.006), 18/19 vs 10/17 at 20% (p = 0.016).** Candidate 11 had
14/17 vs 19/19 (one-sided p = 0.095, "not shown") with the datakit generator. So within the ladder generator, family matters at piece size and size/wholeness matters less (at 40%) than
candidates 9 vs 11 suggested.

Pre-registered post sentence that applies (not shown): *Within one generator (ladder), carved pieces of the unseen families are NOT won more often than the unseen whole grids at 40% (11 of 17 vs 8 of 17; one-sided Fisher p = 0.245).
The whole-vs-piece contrast of candidates 9 vs 11 is therefore not shown to survive a common generator and may be (partly) a generator effect.* With the 20% result (p = 0.016) the fair summary is: the contrast
shrinks at 40% and persists at 20%; neither size nor family alone accounts for everything, consistent with audit findings F1/F2.

## What was done

Pre-registered in `protocol.json` (commit a3645b9, before any generation or evaluation for this control). Option (a) (unseen whole grids through datakit `default_pf`) was ruled out by existing evidence:
the repo's own datakit runs on those whole grids ended `failed` (case2383wp_k after 5,704 s, case2868_rte, case3375wp_k) or with every sample a collapsed root (case2869_pegase 97/97, case2848_rte, case1888_rte, case1951_rte, case3012wp_k)
(`data/external/genco-ladder/data/*/meta.json`). Option (b) was used: **all 36 carved pieces of candidates 10 and 11 (19 seen-family, 17 unseen-family) regenerated through the ladder generator**
(`powerflow_surrogate.run_generate`, 120 usable samples each, seed 0; 35 pieces kept 120 of 120 in conversion, case4837_goc_c2 kept 119 (1 label_mismatch dropped, 117 test scenarios); its rate_a fix was first applied by a manual step, now automated in gen_ladder.py, audit R2-F4) and converted by `ladder_to_datakit` + `pglib_rate_a_fix` exactly as the whole-grid arm was (carved `.m` files staged as `pglib_opf_<piece>.m`).
Every label passes the converter's own check (|dVm| < 1e-6, |dVa| < 1e-4 deg) and my residual check (worst 3.8e-10 pu). All 36 pieces ran (117 to 118 test scenarios each).
Evaluation: GENCO zero-shot, I1-I5, 3 seeds, 40% and 20% noise-free, same code as candidates 9 to 11. Whole-grid results (unseen 17: 8/17 at 40%, 3/17 at 20%; seen 15: 15/15, 13/15) are reused from candidate 9, not rerun.

## Observations

- **The generator matters, in one direction at 40%**: moving the same cut grids from datakit to the ladder generator never helps GENCO at 40% (0 ladder-only wins) and loses 3 pieces, all unseen-family. This is
  the confound the audit flagged, and it is not small relative to the unseen-family effect (14/17 to 11/17).
- **Family at piece size is clearer under the ladder generator** (p = 0.006 vs 0.095). Candidate 11's "NOT SHOWN" for family was partly the generator's softer test.
- PEGASE is the weakest unseen family in both generators (2/6 here, 4/6 with datakit); Polish wins 6/6 in both; RTE moves from 4/5 to 3/5.
- Seen-family whole grids (ladder) already win 15/15 and seen pieces 19/19, so for seen families there is no whole/piece contrast to explain.

## Caveats

- Same as candidates 10 and 11: pieces are boundary-folded, OPF-screened, from few parents (RTE = case2848 only, Polish ~ case2383), not independent draws; unseen whole grids use the PGLib/pandapower networks of candidate 9, not the PGLib carvings.
- The ladder generator scales the folded boundary injections with the load perturbation like any load; this is part of how pieces differ from real subnetworks.
- One generator was used for both arms here (ladder), but the whole-grid arm's 17 unseen grids and the pieces still differ in scenario count and the whole-grid arm's own conversion path details (n per grid).
- Per the audit, the earlier reports' wording was NOT edited here; that is queued for the owner.

## What was not done

- Option (a) (whole grids through datakit): not feasible, evidence above. A cleaner design would generate both arms with datakit on grids where it works.
- Unseen RTE/PEGASE/Polish parents other than those carved in candidate 11; noise and 10% conditions (not requested).
- No audit.
